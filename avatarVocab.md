# Delirio Voice-of-Customer & Competitor Diction Bank
## Ready-to-use language for GLP-1 Meta (Facebook/Instagram) video ads, organized by seven avatars

**TL;DR**
- The single richest, most ad-ready language pool is appearance-and-identity fear language: verbatim customer terms like "skinny fat," "I lost the weight but I have no shape," "the scale isn't moving but my clothes are looser," "my body says not today," and "food noise" — but the most vivid appearance terms ("Ozempic face," "Ozempic body," "deflated," "gaunt") were coined by a dermatologist and popularized by journalists, NOT by patients, so they must be flagged and human-verified before being reproduced as customer voice in an ad.
- No major competitor leads with muscle-preservation in hero copy. Noom's "Muscle Defense™" and Juniper's "muscle maintenance" are the closest; the six biggest telehealth players (Ro, Found, Calibrate, WeightWatchers, Form Health, Simple) do NOT foreground muscle at all — this is Delirio's clearest linguistic white space.
- Match the coach to the fear: **Iris** carries the "some shape"/deflation, plateau/NSV, and longevity/menopause avatars; **Reed** carries the DEXA/data muscle-worrier, the dose-ramp consistency-faller, and the "lock in / protect your gains" regain-fearer. Test both against the gym-terrified beginner.

## Key Findings
1. **Verbatim customer language clusters around loss, not gain.** Real users describe the fear ("deflated," "soft," "skinny fat," "no shape") far more vividly than the goal. Ads should mirror the fear in the hook, then pivot to Delirio's mechanism (real-time form correction + between-session follow-up).
2. **A shared jargon set signals in-group membership** — "food noise," "NSV / non-scale victory," "recomp," "anabolic not catabolic," "gymtimidation," "lean mass," "muscle preservation." Using it correctly buys instant credibility; using it wrong marks the advertiser as an outsider.
3. **Muscle-preservation is under-claimed by competitors**, so Delirio can own "keep the muscle / train correctly" language without fighting an incumbent.
4. **"Beyond the scale" is crowded** (Ro, Found, Hers all use it). Delirio should differentiate on form-correction and consistency, not generic "beyond the scale."
5. **Compliance guardrail:** keep every claim on training, muscle, strength, form, and consistency — never on drug outcomes or guaranteed medical results.

Provenance is tagged throughout: **[C]** = verbatim/near-verbatim customer voice; **[B]** = brand/marketing language (competitive intel, do not copy verbatim); **[J]** = journalist/clinician-origin framing (verify before quoting as customer voice).

## AVATAR 1 — The Skinny-Fat Panicker (38–55, mostly women, 20–50 lbs down)

**(a) Vocabulary / phrase list**
- "skinny fat" **[C + category]** — the dominant self-label; appears in patient forums and in trainer/press content alike.
- "Ozempic body" **[J — FLAG]** — coined by NYC cosmetic dermatologist **Dr. Paul Jarrod Frank**, who told CNN (July 30, 2025) that "Ozempic face" is often accompanied by a phenomenon he dubbed "Ozempic body": "One of the other major side effects of weight loss… is muscle loss. And we see this all over the body." Not organic patient coinage.
- "deflated," "soft," "saggy," "smaller but not better," "no shape" — **"deflated" is largely clinician/journalist framing [J — FLAG]** (plastic surgeons describe the "deflated or loose appearance" when fat pads shrink faster than skin retracts); "soft"/"no shape" read as genuine customer voice **[C]**.
- "want some shape," "want to be toned," "toned not deflated" **[C]**
- "Ozempic butt," "Ozempic face" **[J — FLAG]** — "Ozempic face" was coined by Dr. Paul Jarrod Frank in *The New York Times* (Jan. 24, 2023, "Those Weight Loss Drugs May Do a Number on Your Face"); he said "I see it every day in my office."

**(b) Full quotable sayings (customer voice) [C]**
- "I'm thinner but I don't look better."
- "I lost the weight but I have no shape."
- "I'm skinny fat now."
- "I wanted to be toned, not deflated."

**(c) Emotional driver:** Grief and a sense of betrayal — they did the hard thing and the mirror didn't reward them. Underneath: fear of looking sick, older, or "gaunt."

**(d) Coach fit:** **Iris** — "let's build some shape back," strength-as-shape framing.

## AVATAR 2 — The Muscle-Loss Worrier (35–60, research-driven)

**(a) Vocabulary**
- "lean mass," "LBM," "fat-free mass," "DEXA," "InBody," "body recomp / recomposition" **[C]**
- "anabolic not catabolic," "muscle preservation," "sarcopenia," "sarcopenic obesity" **[C + category]**
- **The muscle-loss stat** — commonly stated by users as "25–40% of weight loss is muscle." Grounded in real data: in the STEP-1 semaglutide trial (*NEJM*, 2021) roughly **39%** of weight lost was lean mass; the SURMOUNT-1 tirzepatide substudy found roughly **25%** (Neeland, Linge & Birkenfeld, 2024, *Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism*). A 2025 Endocrine Society presentation (ENDO 2025; Dr. Melanie Haines, Massachusetts General Hospital/Harvard; 40 adults over 3 months) reported "approximately 40% of the weight lost from taking semaglutide… comes from lean mass, including muscle." Use the range, not a single scary number, and attribute it.
- "protect my metabolism," "muscle is my metabolic sink," "muscle is resilience" **[C / expert-influenced]**

**(b) Full quotable sayings [C]**
- "I plan to keep close track with DEXA scans and up my exercise."
- "The challenge was stuffing myself with enough protein — 120g."
- "I want to lose fat, not muscle."
- "Up to 40% of what you lose can be muscle." (echoing the ENDO 2025 / Endocrine Society figure — attribute, don't imply it's universal)

**(c) Emotional driver:** Control through data; fear of an invisible loss (muscle) they can't see in the mirror until it's too late.

**(d) Coach fit:** **Reed** — data-driven, "protect your lean mass," progressive-overload language, "make the weight you lose fat, not muscle."

## CROSS-CUTTING CATEGORY JARGON GLOSSARY
- **Skinny fat** — thin but soft/untoned; low muscle, relatively high fat. **[C + category]**
- **Ozempic body / Ozempic face / Ozempic butt** — appearance after rapid GLP-1 loss; "Ozempic face" and "Ozempic body" both coined by dermatologist Dr. Paul Jarrod Frank (NYT 2023; CNN 2025). **[J — FLAG]**
- **Deflated / gaunt / hollow / sunken / crepey** — appearance terms; "deflated" and "gaunt" originate largely in dermatology/plastic-surgery and press framing. **[J — FLAG]**
- **Food noise** — intrusive, constant thoughts about food; popularized 2024 (Oprah special). **[C]**
- **NSV / non-scale victory** — progress the scale can't show. **[C + brand]**
- **Recomp / recomposition** — losing fat and building muscle simultaneously. **[C + category]**
- **Anabolic vs. catabolic** — building vs. breaking down tissue. **[C]**
- **Lean mass / LBM / fat-free mass** — muscle-inclusive non-fat body mass. **[C + clinical]**
- **Muscle preservation / muscle maintenance** — retaining muscle during loss. **[C + brand]**
- **Sarcopenia / sarcopenic obesity** — age/loss-related muscle loss; muscle loss with retained fat. **[clinical → C]**
- **DEXA / InBody** — body-composition scans. **[C]**
- **Gymtimidation** — gym anxiety/intimidation. **[C + category]**
- **Titrating / dose ramp** — increasing GLP-1 dose over time. **[C + clinical]**
- **Ozempic rebound / regain** — weight returning after stopping. **[C + category]**
- **Set point** — the weight the body defends. **[clinical → C, also used by Ro]**
- **Toned / some shape / strong not skinny** — aspirational appearance goals. **[C]**

## Recommendations
1. **Lead hooks with verbatim customer fear language, then pivot to Delirio's mechanism.** E.g., open on "I lost the weight but I have no shape" → Iris: "Let's build some shape back — I'll watch your form so you never have to guess." Stage the whole ad library this way, one avatar quote per creative.
2. **Own the muscle-preservation white space.** No telehealth leader claims it in hero copy. Use "keep the muscle, not just lose the weight" and "train correctly so the weight you lose is fat, not muscle." Only Noom, Juniper, and Gym Rookie contest this lane — none with real-time camera form correction.
3. **For Avatar 3, weaponize the private/at-home angle** against gymtimidation: "No gym. No eyes on you. Just your coach in your pocket telling you exactly what to do." This directly answers "I have no idea what to do in there" and "everyone's watching."
4. **For Avatar 5, use energy-aware language:** "Your body says not today? We'll adjust. On shot day we go lighter — not zero." Mirror the real user ritual of workout → shot → rest.
5. **Segment creative by coach:** Iris for Avatars 1, 4, 7 (women, shape, plateau, longevity); Reed for Avatars 2, 5, 6 (data, gains, consistency, regain). A/B both on Avatar 3.
6. **Do NOT copy Calibrate's anti-AI stance.** Instead reframe: an AI coach that "remembers everything, never judges, and is there at 6am on shot day" — turn Delirio's AI nature into the accountability advantage Future and Simple hint at.

**Benchmarks that change the plan:**
- If empowerment-quote ads ("I'm training for 80") underperform appearance-anxiety ads ("skinny fat," "no shape") on CTR by a meaningful margin, shift budget toward the fear-first angle — but keep appearance claims non-medical.
- If Meta or legal flags medical claims, strip ALL drug-outcome and body-composition-percentage language and run pure training/strength/form creative.
- If the camera-form-correction demo (the "your left knee is caving" moment) beats talking-head coach ads, make the live-correction demo the default creative format across all avatars — it is Delirio's single defensible differentiator vs. Zing and Gym Rookie.

## Caveats
- **Verbatim vs. brand vs. journalist provenance:** Customer-voice quotes **[C]** are the ad gold. Brand phrases **[B]** are competitive intel — do not reproduce verbatim (trademark/passing-off risk, e.g., Noom's "Muscle Defense™"). "Ozempic face," "Ozempic body," "deflated," and "gaunt" **[J]** are dermatologist/journalist coinages (Dr. Paul Jarrod Frank; plastic-surgery and press sources) — confirm a real customer uses the word before presenting it as customer voice.
- **Reddit / forum verification:** Specific permalinks are hard to verify and many quotes here surfaced via aggregators, press round-ups, or drug-review sites rather than confirmed original posts. Human-verify any exact quote against a live, attributable source before public reproduction in a paid ad.
- **Statistic hygiene:** The "25–40% of weight loss is muscle" figure is real but range-dependent and trial-dependent (STEP-1 ~39% lean mass; SURMOUNT-1 ~25%; ENDO 2025 ~40%). Attribute it and use the range; do not present a single alarming number as universal fact.
- **Compliance:** Avoid any implication of guaranteed medical outcomes. Keep Delirio's claims to training, form, muscle, strength, and consistency. Note Juniper's UK ASA ruling as a cautionary precedent for advertising near prescription-only medicines.
