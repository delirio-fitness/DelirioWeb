# GLP-1 Content Opportunity — Delirio

**Last updated:** 2026-09-04

## The opportunity

GLP-1 medications (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound) are the fastest-growing drug category in the US. As of 2026, prescriptions continue to climb, and every new user eventually asks: **"How should I exercise on this medication?"**

The current content landscape for this question is:
- **Medical sites** (Healthline, Mayo Clinic, WebMD): Generic advice like "exercise is important while on GLP-1." Not actionable. No specific plans.
- **News articles** (NYT, CNN): One-off features, not evergreen content.
- **Reddit threads** (r/Ozempic, r/Semaglutide): Anecdotal user experiences, no structured guidance.
- **Fitness apps and coaches**: **Almost nothing.** This is the gap.

PubMed confirms the research is accelerating: publications on GLP-1 + exercise + strength training went from 1 paper in 2021 to 13 in 2026. The science is catching up, but consumer-facing fitness content has not.

### The key concerns of GLP-1 users about exercise

From research and community analysis, these are the primary worries:

1. **Muscle loss** — "Am I losing muscle along with fat?" (The #1 concern — research confirms 20-40% of weight lost on GLP-1 can be lean mass without resistance training)
2. **Energy changes** — "I have no energy to work out on this medication" (Common side effect, especially early)
3. **Not knowing where to start** — "I've never strength trained, and now my doctor says I need to"
4. **Adapting when things change** — Energy, appetite, weight, and strength fluctuate week to week on GLP-1
5. **Maintaining after stopping** — "What happens to my fitness when I taper off?"
6. **Is it safe?** — "Can I do intense exercise on Ozempic?"

## Medical and legal guardrails

Per Delirio's claims-ledger.md and product beliefs:

- **Never claim Delirio treats, prevents, or manages any medical condition**

- **Cite published research** — PubMed has 34 relevant papers as of September 2026
