# Delirio UI/UX evidence and market reference packet

Last research pass: **2026-07-12**. This is the reusable audit trail for `ui-guru` and `ux-guru`, not a substitute for their constitutions. Recheck live competitor, Apple, legal, and platform sources before decisions because those can change.

## How to read the evidence

- **Observed** means directly visible or explicitly stated in the linked first-party experience on the observation date.
- **Reported** means stated by a participant, reviewer, researcher, or third party.
- **Inferred** is a Delirio-specific interpretation, not a proven cause of usability, trust, or conversion.
- Competitor use of a pattern is not evidence that the pattern works. It is category context to test against Delirio's users.

## Foundation: standards and original research

### Human-like AI and calibrated trust

- **Cohn et al., “Believing Anthropomorphism: Examining the Role of Anthropomorphic Cues on User Trust in Large Language Models,” CHI 2024.** [Google Research publication](https://research.google/pubs/believing-anthropomorphism-examining-the-role-of-anthropomorphic-cues-on-user-trust-in-large-language-models/) · [full author PDF](https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/7813.pdf) · DOI [10.1145/3613905.3650818](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613905.3650818). Relevant: abstract; §§2.2, 3.1, 4.3, 5; Figures 2–3. In an online US experiment with 2,165 adults, speech plus text increased anthropomorphism and perceived information accuracy; first-person effects varied by scenario. Supports treating Delirio's voice, avatars, names, and first-person language as trust-bearing cues and pairing them with visible limits. Limit: pseudo-LLM scenarios were not fitness coaching or a longitudinal relationship, and perceived accuracy is not actual accuracy. **Access: full 15-page paper reviewed.**

- **Amershi et al., “Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction,” CHI 2019.** [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/) · [full PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf) · DOI [10.1145/3290605.3300233](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300233). Relevant: Table 1 on pp. 3–4 and appendix examples on pp. 14–18, especially G1–G2 and G7–G11. The 18 guidelines were synthesized from more than 150 recommendations and evaluated with 49 practitioners across 20 AI products. Supports capability/error expectation, invocation, dismissal, correction, graceful uncertainty, explanation, memory, and feedback contracts. Limit: general design guidance, not a clinical or fitness safety validation. **Access: full 19-page paper reviewed.**

- **NIST, AI Risk Management Framework 1.0, Appendix C: Human-AI Interaction, 2023.** [Official appendix](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/appendices/app-c-ai-risk-management-and-human-ai-interaction/) · [framework PDF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf). Relevant: Appendix C points 2–4 and Govern/Map discussion. Supports defining human/AI roles, evaluating context-specific behavior with impacted communities, and avoiding the assumption that one explanation fits every person. Limit: voluntary risk framework, not a component specification. **Access: official appendix reviewed; framework PDF available.**

### Voice timing and conversational control

- **Stivers et al., “Universals and cultural variation in turn-taking in conversation,” PNAS 2009.** [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2705608/) · DOI [10.1073/pnas.0903616106](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0903616106). Relevant: Results/Discussion and response-time distributions across ten languages. Natural conversation tends to minimize gap and overlap, with cultural variation and meaningful delays. Supports measuring gap, overlap, interruption, and delayed-response meaning; the commonly cited roughly 200 ms gap is context, not a product SLA. Limit: human informal conversation, not noisy workouts or synthetic agents. **Access: full paper available and reviewed through PMC/PDF text.**

- **Google, “Learn about conversation” / Conversation Design.** [Official guide](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/learn-about-conversation). Relevant: `Turn-taking`, `Ask questions`, and `Don't monopolize`, plus linked Method Podcast episode 8 excerpts at 2:37 and 3:13. Supports explicit turn handoffs, one question at a time, brevity, and not speaking after asking. The page links a timestamped first-party podcast about designing speech to fit human conversation. **Access: guide and its timestamped excerpt text reviewed; linked YouTube/audio playback was blocked by redirect/cookie controls, so the recording was not watched or heard.**

### Dark-theme readability and energy limits

- **Dobres, Chahine, and Reimer, “Effects of ambient illumination, contrast polarity, and letter size on text legibility under glance-like reading,” Applied Ergonomics 60 (2017), 68–73.** [Full author PDF](https://jdobr.es/pdf/Dobres-etal-2017-Ambient.pdf) · DOI [10.1016/j.apergo.2016.11.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apergo.2016.11.001). Relevant: introduction pp. 1–2, results/discussion pp. 4–5. The study found a positive-polarity advantage under glance-like reading, with greater negative-polarity cost under dark ambient conditions and small text. Supports extra care for Delirio's small labels, transcripts, FAQ, legal, and status text while preserving the dark-first product constraint. Limit: lexical identification on a desktop display, not sustained reading on every modern phone or every visual ability. **Access: full six-page paper reviewed.**

- **Wan et al./Purdue Engineering, “Shedding light on dark mode to save energy,” 2021/2022 study summary.** [University summary](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ECE/News/2022/shedding-light-on-dark-mode-to-save-energy). Relevant: normal indoor auto-brightness comparison and brightness/content interaction. Researchers reported that normal-use total-phone savings from switching OLED phones to dark mode were much smaller than popular claims, while savings grew at high brightness. Supports not forcing pure black or claiming major battery benefits without Delirio-specific measurement. Limit: selected Android/OLED devices and workloads; not an iPhone-specific Delirio benchmark. **Access: university research summary reviewed; linked paper should be reopened for energy-specific implementation work.**

- **Jerónimo and Carraça, “Effects of fitspiration content on body image: a systematic review,” Eating and Weight Disorders 27 (2022), 3017–3035.** [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9676749/) · DOI [10.1007/s40519-022-01505-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40519-022-01505-4). ### Choice architecture, information structure, and experiments

- **Scheibehenne, Greifeneder, and Todd, “Can There Ever Be Too Many Options? A Meta-Analytic Review of Choice Overload,” Journal of Consumer Research 37 (2010), 409–425.** [Full author PDF](https://scheibehenne.com/ScheibehenneGreifenederTodd2010.pdf) · DOI [10.1086/651235](https://doi.org/10.1086/651235). Relevant: method pp. 4–6, results pp. 7–10, moderators/discussion pp. 11–14. Across 63 conditions from 50 experiments (N=5,036), the mean choice-overload effect was virtually zero with substantial variance. Supports not hiding one of two Delirio coaches behind a generic “fewer choices” rule; differentiation, defaults, and order should be tested in context. Limit: consumer-choice tasks varied widely and did not study AI coach personas. **Access: full 17-page paper reviewed.**

- **W3C WAI, “Writing for Web Accessibility” and “Headings.”** [Writing guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/writing/) · [Headings tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/page-structure/headings/). Relevant: meaningful headings, relevant information first, and headings as a navigable outline. Supports FAQ question wording, first-sentence answers, stable hierarchy, and content that works for scanners and assistive technology. **Access: official pages reviewed.**

- **Dmitriev, Gupta, Kim, and Vaz, “A Dirty Dozen: Twelve Common Metric Interpretation Pitfalls in Online Controlled Experiments,” KDD 2017.** [Microsoft Research page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/a-dirty-dozen-twelve-common-metric-interpretation-pitfalls-in-online-controlled-experiments/) · [full PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2020/08/2017-08-KDDMetricInterpretationPitfalls.pdf). Relevant: metric taxonomy §4 and pitfalls §5, especially underpowered metrics and borderline p-values. Supports predeclared primary/guardrail/data-quality metrics, power analysis, confidence intervals, and avoiding “no effect” claims from underpowered tests. **Access: full ten-page paper reviewed.**

## Contrary and limiting evidence

- Dark-first is a user/product constant, but the polarity literature does **not** establish it as universally more legible. Keep the dark theme while testing actual reading and override modes; do not convert the Dobres study into a mandate for a light redesign.
- Cohn et al. show changes in **perceived** accuracy and risk, not that every anthropomorphic cue always causes overtrust or that personality should be removed. The correct response is calibrated disclosure and agency, followed by Delirio-specific comprehension testing.
- The choice-overload meta-analysis found a near-zero average effect and large heterogeneity. It challenges “fewer is always better,” but it does not prove every two-choice layout is clear or unbiased; defaults, order, descriptions, and task still matter.
- The fitspiration review reported concerning patterns but had population, language, heterogeneity, and review-process limitations. Use it to avoid gratuitous appearance pressure and to test inclusive alternatives, not to claim all athletic imagery harms everyone.
- Competitor observations reveal conventions and objections, not causality. No competitor analytics, usability studies, conversion rates, accessibility audits, or private product flows were available in this pass.
- No Delirio-specific eye-tracking study, gaze heatmap, usability benchmark, conversion baseline, validated audience study, localization plan, or measured voice-latency distribution was found in the workspace. The audience brief and map are strategic hypotheses, not observed conversion or retention evidence. Generic eye-tracking patterns must not be presented as Delirio heatmaps.

## Unresolved uncertainty and next evidence

- **Which GLP-1 subsegment becomes the initial wedge?** The founder has selected GLP-1 users as Delirio's first target market. Midlife, time-constrained professional women are the lead persona within that market, while the strongest medication phase and behavioral need remain unvalidated. Test qualified conversion, willingness to pay, retention intent, GLP-1 phase, prior coaching, and training history. Men and non-GLP-1 visitors remain useful adjacent signals, not equal first-priority acquisition audiences.
- **What voice timing does Delirio actually deliver?** Instrument end-of-speech, first meaningful audio, full-turn completion, barge-in cutoff, overlap, retry, and repeat utterance at p50/p75/p95 on representative iPhones and networks before setting thresholds.
- **Can Delirio substantiate current marketing claims?** Build the claim ledger and resolve the exact $50/month, competitor price bands, “for most people,” “always responsive,” memory, and real-time form-correction scope with product/engineering/legal owners.
- **Is the App Store funnel healthy?** Monitor the verified product link and use privacy-safe downstream purchase signals where available; a website click remains only an outbound-intent event.
- **Which coach-choice presentation is fairest and clearest?** Run task-based tests with active choice vs. labeled preview, reverse order, and equal sample prompts. Measure comprehension, choice confidence, switch behavior, and position/default bias before optimizing share.
- **What layout attracts the right attention?** First run task-based mobile/desktop usability and comprehension tests. Eye tracking is optional only after a concrete unresolved attention question; it must use Delirio pages, representative tasks, and concurrent outcome measures rather than borrowing third-party heatmaps.
- **Which locales ship first?** Before claiming support, select locales, commission professional translation/cultural review, test scripts and RTL, and reconcile marketing/FAQ/legal wording. Pseudo-localization only proves structural resilience.
