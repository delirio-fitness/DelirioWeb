# Delirio product and audience beliefs

**Last synthesized:** 2026-07-28  
**Status:** Working product constitution. GLP-1-first targeting is an explicit founder decision; needs, positioning, and subsegment assumptions within that market remain hypotheses to test.

This is the compact operational constitution. The complete research, content model, page sequence, copy direction, claims boundaries, evidence, sources, and hive synthesis are consolidated in [`docs/GLP1_CONTENT_MOMENTUM_STRATEGY.md`](../docs/GLP1_CONTENT_MOMENTUM_STRATEGY.md), which is the canonical final strategy file.

This document distills the durable decisions from the current DelirioWeb implementation, the audience-language brief, the audience map, competitor research, and the product-design conversation. It is the shared source of truth for `ui-guru` and `ux-guru`. Experiments may change. The principles below should only change when new product evidence or an explicit product decision supersedes them.

## Product model

- Delirio is an iOS product built around a continuing relationship with an AI fitness coach, represented by Iris or Reed.
- The coach owns the planning burden. It uses the user's goal, schedule, time, energy, equipment, training history, and recent sessions to prepare and adapt the plan. Never frame the core value as asking the user to plan her own training.
- The user's role is agency without administrative burden: set the goal, provide context, choose or correct, show up, start, pause, return, and continue.
- Coaching spans the whole experience: planning before a session, voice or text guidance, live workout support where the product can observe reliably, post-workout interpretation, and continuity into the next session.
- The differentiator is not a feature list. It is continuity with context: fewer restarts, less figuring out, guidance at the moment of uncertainty, and a next step already informed by what happened.
- A week is a useful planning frame because it matches schedules and missed-day recovery. It is not a rigid promise. Copy should allow today, this session, next time, and changing weeks to coexist.

## Audience portfolio and targeting hierarchy

Delirio is GLP-1-first. Product prioritization, acquisition, research, and homepage decisions should begin with the needs of people using, maintaining, tapering, or coming off GLP-1 medication. When an unresolved tradeoff exists between broad fitness appeal and a clearer GLP-1 use case, choose the GLP-1 use case unless safety, evidence, accessibility, or observed user data argues otherwise.

1. People using GLP-1 medication who want to build or maintain strength, protect functional capability, create a sustainable training routine, and keep continuity while weight, appetite, energy, confidence, or schedule changes.
2. People approaching a goal, maintaining results, tapering, or stopping medication who need a credible next chapter rather than another short-term plan.
3. Midlife, time-constrained professional women are the lead initial persona within the GLP-1 market. Gender, age, or occupation must not become an eligibility assumption or erase other GLP-1 users.
4. People with externally dependent consistency and people returning after online or in-person coaching are important behavioral subsegments within the primary audience.
5. Non-GLP-1 visitors remain welcome and should understand the universal continuity value, but they are an adjacent expansion audience rather than an equal first acquisition target.

Do not prioritize people with no demonstrated desire to train, or people whose training consistency is already self-sustaining, in primary acquisition messaging. This is a prioritization hypothesis, not a statement about who may use the product.

Important GLP-1 contexts are materially different:

- Active weight loss can raise anxiety about strength and body composition.
- Approaching a goal shifts attention toward what comes next.
- Maintenance can create long-lived concern about regain and consistency.
- Tapering or forced discontinuation can create acute uncertainty about preserving progress.

Never present Delirio as prescribing, diagnosing, treating, replacing a clinician, guaranteeing muscle preservation, or determining medication use. Fitness guidance must remain inside the product's demonstrated capability.

## Audience psychology and emotional job

The audience usually knows exercise matters. Delirio does not need to lecture them into wanting fitness. The more valuable job is resolving the gap between intention and sustained action.

Primary frictions:

- lack of accountability and follow-through
- lack of organization, time, and schedule support
- uncertainty about what to do and whether it is appropriate
- motivation that falls when life changes
- repeated planning burden and decision fatigue
- gym intimidation or low starting confidence
- shame after lapses and the belief that another restart proves personal failure
- generic tools that do not remember context or adapt meaningfully
- human coaching that can be expensive, difficult to schedule, or unavailable between appointments

Desired emotional movement:

- uncertain to clear
- overwhelmed to guided
- inconsistent to steady
- self-conscious to capable
- behind to in motion
- isolated effort to continuous support
- another restart to a supported return
- generic instruction to relevant guidance

The central emotional promise is: **You are not starting over. You are continuing from where you are now, with guidance that understands what happened and helps you move forward.**

## Instantaneous conversion model

The central business node is **instantaneous conversion**: helping a qualified visitor reach confident action during the current visit rather than leaving with unresolved questions. This is not permission to manufacture urgency or pressure. It means the page should answer the visitor's conversion questions quickly, concretely, and in the right order.

Do not collapse every action into the same conversion. Distinguish:

- commercial intent: opening the verified App Store listing
- product engagement: trying or exploring the coach experience
- research participation: completing “Shape What’s Next”
- actual acquisition or subscription: a downstream outcome that a website click alone does not prove

The conversion graph has seven decision branches:

### 1. Present relevance: Do I care?

The visitor asks whether the problem bothers her now, creates friction, complicates daily life, or makes training harder to sustain. Reflect recognizable moments such as repeatedly figuring out what to do, losing momentum after schedule changes, feeling uncertain during training, or having no useful support between appointments. Do not begin with abstract product capability before establishing a lived problem.

### 2. Differentiation: Is this product meaningfully different?

Show the mechanism, not the adjective. Delirio is different when one AI coach prepares the plan, responds through voice or text, supports the workout, remembers relevant context, and adjusts what comes next. Product screenshots, working interaction states, and specific before/during/after continuity are stronger than “personalized” or “AI-powered.”

### 3. Consequence: Is the problem important enough?

Help the visitor understand what unresolved inconsistency or missing guidance costs in her own life: repeated restarts, wasted time, unused subscriptions or equipment, uncertainty, lost confidence, and difficulty sustaining strength work. For GLP-1 audiences, body-composition or strength concerns may be relevant, but medical, muscle-loss, injury, or long-term health consequences require direct evidence and careful qualification. Never inflate severity merely to make the product feel necessary.

### 4. Feasibility and timing: Why is now workable?

Answer whether the product fits the visitor's current finances, schedule, equipment, and readiness. Pricing must be visible and interpretable before the outbound purchase action. Present monthly and annual choices without hiding tradeoffs, and provide a clear contact path when price or fit is uncertain. “Now” should mean the product fits present circumstances, not that a fake deadline exists.

### 5. Cost of delay: Does waiting compound the problem?

Show honest compounding effects that Delirio can substantiate, such as another week of decision friction, another lapse becoming harder to return from, or unresolved planning continuing to consume attention. Do not imply inevitable medical decline, rapid muscle loss, injury, regain, or worsening health without evidence. Prefer loss of continuity and accumulated friction over fear-based health urgency.

### 6. Personal recognition: Does the product see me?

Use language and scenarios that recognize the visitor's schedule, training history, confidence, equipment, interruptions, and current GLP-1 context where relevant. Recognition comes from specificity and respect, not repeatedly saying “for you.” The product should feel schedule-aware, nonjudgmental, adult, and useful without inferring personal facts the visitor did not provide.

### 7. Identity and belonging: Are people like me welcome here?

Representation should include people who resemble the likely audience in age, body size, training experience, environment, and confidence. Avoid showing only young, highly trained, very lean athletes. A visitor should be able to imagine herself using Delirio without feeling that she must already look fit enough to belong.

Representation is not fabricated social proof. Do not label models, stock subjects, or generated people as customers. Existing-customer language, testimonials, outcomes, and demographic claims require real permission and evidence. Include men and non-GLP-1 users without weakening the women-first acquisition hypothesis.

### Applying the graph to page structure

The seven questions do not require seven literal sections. A strong page can answer several in one content unit:

- Hero: present relevance, recognition, and the core continuity promise.
- Product journey: differentiation and consequence through concrete mechanics.
- Coach voice/text experience: differentiation, personal recognition, and trust.
- Audience imagery: identity and belonging without fabricated customer claims.
- AI trust and FAQ: consequence calibration, capability limits, and objections.
- Pricing: financial feasibility and timing.
- App Store and questionnaire actions: commercial conversion or research participation.

Audit every major section against at least one decision branch. Remove sections that only repeat claims without resolving a new conversion question.

## Language and content contract

- Voice: direct, warm, adult, composed, observant, precise, supportive, physically literate, and nonjudgmental.
- Use clear observation, useful meaning, and a practical next step.
- Favor strength, capability, clarity, confidence, consistency, continuity, control, progress, guidance, adaptation, trust, support, momentum, return, and what comes next.
- Make progress concrete. Prefer “Your next session has been adjusted” over transformation language.
- Respect intelligence. Explain why something changed, expose estimates and uncertainty, and let the user correct the coach.
- Reduce pressure. Avoid “no excuses,” “never miss,” “push through,” punishment, guilt, shame, and discipline-as-morality language.
- Do not describe the audience as old, fragile, lazy, broken, vain, technologically naive, or needing her old body back.
- Do not use idealized “new you,” before-and-after, body-shaming, or fitspiration framing.
- Avoid vague category filler such as “AI-powered,” “personalized,” “holistic,” “revolutionary,” or “seamless” unless the following copy makes the mechanism specific.
- New marketing copy must not use em dashes. Use periods, commas, colons, parentheses, or sentence breaks.
- The website should make GLP-1 relevance discoverable early without turning every sentence into medication copy. Lead with shared needs such as strength, consistency, changing energy, and what comes next, then include explicit GLP-1 context at high-intent or trust-sensitive moments. The conditional questionnaire and targeted campaigns should deepen phase-specific learning.

## Current acquisition and research journey

- The header's primary outbound action is the official Apple App Store badge, not an ambiguous “meet your coach” button.
- The default hero is Hero V3. It uses the audience-led message that the user sets the goal and the coach helps her show up, start, and continue.
- “Shape What’s Next” opens a research questionnaire. It does not promise to generate a workout or plan on the spot.
- The questionnaire is for customer discovery and funnel-signal collection. Its answers may guide what Delirio builds and may end in an App Store download action.
- The GLP-1 context question comes first because the primary market contains materially different medication phases and needs. Other experience and motivation questions should branch when a prior answer changes whether the next question is relevant.
- Prefer three choices. Four is the maximum under normal conditions. Use one answer per question, immediate selection feedback, a brief stable pause, then automatic progression. Keep Back and Close predictable.
- Do not reveal an intimidating fixed total. The current design uses a full-width progress bar without a numeric fraction.
- Every questionnaire invocation starts a fresh response. Repeat participation is allowed and creates a new Firestore submission rather than overwriting a prior response.
- Closing the modal only closes it. It must not unexpectedly navigate the page.

## AI trust and safety position

- Iris and Reed are AI fitness coaches. Name the AI relationship clearly where selection and reliance occur.
- Address AI skepticism holistically across planning, text advice, voice sessions, live workout guidance, memory, model improvement, and stress testing. Do not imply reliability is only a real-time form-feedback concern.
- Explain the system as tested, tuned, and retested within defined tasks and limits. Never use unrelated medical-diagnostic performance as proof that Delirio itself is accurate.
- Show what the coach knows, what input it used, what it could not assess, why a recommendation changed, and how the user can correct, dismiss, retry, switch modes, or stop.
- Never imply consciousness, a human credential, medical authority, continuous observation, perfect accuracy, injury prevention, diagnosis, therapy, or emotional dependence.
- Supportive warmth is valuable. Fabricated care, fake expertise, urgency pressure, and trust-by-aesthetic are not.
- Health and fitness claims require a claim ledger with evidence, scope, qualifier, owner, and review date before visual emphasis.

## Visual beliefs

- The current brand is dark-first, spacious, premium, energetic, and human. Light sections are allowed, but they must be full-bleed rows without accidental black gutters.
- Exo 2 is the default landing-page type family. Use expressive scale for short headings and legible weights, measures, and line heights for body copy, chat, FAQ, pricing, forms, and legal content.
- Soft green is a scarce continuity and action accent. It can connect important motivational words, live status, or key functional moments, but must not be applied to every CTA or every carousel step.
- Reed blue and Iris pink identify coach personality. Status, success, failure, safety, and selection must never depend on those colors alone.
- Product screenshots use one shared matte iPhone frame with minimal shine. Do not mix frame geometries inside one comparison or carousel.
- Official Apple artwork stays unmodified. Use the Apple-supplied master at its intrinsic aspect ratio, currently 120 by 40 CSS pixels in the header and approximately 150 by 50 in the footer, subject to Apple's clear-space rules and a verified Delirio listing.
- Hero media and product screenshots must support the message rather than become generic athletic decoration. Reserve prior hero experiments and scoreboards as reusable dormant components instead of forcing irrelevant statistics into the default hero.
- Spacing is part of the product system. Use layout `gap`, section padding, and content-led sizing. Do not use arbitrary offsets to repair one viewport. Validate spacing in a real browser.

## Experience beliefs

- The page narrative is: recognize the problem, understand the coach relationship, see how it works, experience voice or text, understand continuity and limits, resolve price and trust questions, then download.
- Demonstrate the relationship before relying on abstract AI claims.
- Voice and text are peers. Voice can be the default after an explicit coach/session action, but switching to Text must end the voice session and preserve understandable context.
- Connection status must be stable and honest. Retry up to the defined limit, debounce visible transitions, require a healthy interval before claiming connection, and provide a clear Retry action after failure.
- Message history and the composer are visibly separate. Sending, responding, failure, and retry states live near the conversation they describe.
- Carousels must not hide essential meaning. The vertical product journey keeps direct chapter controls and must remain understandable with motion disabled and on mobile.
- Responsive work is not proportional shrinking alone. Preserve hierarchy, readable type, touch targets, content order, screenshot containment, and intentional space at 320, 390 by 844, 820 by 1180, 1440 by 900, and larger desktop viewports.
- Use full-viewport height only where the narrative benefits and content remains visible without clipping. Other sections should grow from content rather than inherit a standardized fixed height.

## Decision hierarchy

When instructions conflict, decide in this order:

1. Safety, truthful capability, privacy, and accessibility.
2. Product ownership model: the coach handles planning while the user retains agency.
3. Clear comprehension and recovery across real devices.
4. Audience relevance and emotional respect.
5. Conversion clarity and research quality.
6. Visual novelty.

## Open questions that require evidence

- Which GLP-1 phase and behavioral subsegment produces the strongest qualified conversion, willingness-to-pay, and retention signal?
- Which GLP-1 needs are sufficiently shared to lead the main homepage, and which require phase-specific landing pages or onboarding?
- Does explicit GLP-1 homepage context improve qualified conversion and trust while keeping the universal continuity story understandable to adjacent users?
- Which coach-choice presentation produces informed selection rather than default or position bias?
- What are Delirio's actual voice latency, retry, interruption, and session-success distributions?
- Which coaching, form-analysis, memory, and adaptation claims can be substantiated in production?
- Does the questionnaire improve learning and downstream App Store intent without degrading trust?

Prefer instrumentation and user evidence over taste when resolving these questions.
